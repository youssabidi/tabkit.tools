UUID Versions at a Glance

  • Version 1: Date-time and MAC address based (exposes device hardware).
  • Version 4: 100% pseudo-random cryptographic generation (industry gold standard).
  • Version 5: SHA-1 namespace and named string hashing.
  • Version 7: Unix timestamp prefix with random suffix (ideal for B-Tree indexes).

Collision Probability & Math

A version 4 UUID consists of 32 hexadecimal digits across 5 groups: 8-4-4-4-12. 6 bits are reserved for variant and version, leaving 122 bits of true entropy:

Total Unique UUIDs = 2^122 ≈ 5.31 × 10^36

UUID v4 Structure, Collision Odds & Database Indexing

Version 4 UUIDs pack 122 bits of cryptographic randomness alongside 6 fixed version/variant bits into the canonical 8-4-4-4-12 hexadecimal format.

  • Version & Variant Nibbles: In xxxxxxxx-xxxx-4xxx-yxxx-xxxxxxxxxxxx, the third group always starts with "4" (version 4) and the fourth group starts with 8, 9, a, or b (RFC 4122 variant).
  • Astronomically Low Collision Risk: With 2^122 (~5.3×10^36) random states, generating one billion UUIDs every second for 85 years yields negligible collision probability.
  • B-Tree Fragmentation Tip: Random UUID v4 values make great opaque public IDs, whereas massive append-heavy Postgres/MySQL primary keys often pair internal sequential BIGINT/UUIDv7 keys with public v4 slugs to minimize page splits.
