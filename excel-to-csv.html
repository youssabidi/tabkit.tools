Multi-Sheet Exports & UTF-8 CSV Rules

Unlike .xlsx, plain CSV stores only a single flat grid of values without formulas, cell colors, or multiple tabs.

  • Evaluated Formula Values: Converting Excel to CSV writes the cached evaluated cell results (numbers/text) rather than underlying =SUM() formulas.
  • Choosing Tab vs. Comma: When product descriptions contain unquoted commas or HTML fragments, Tab-delimited (.tsv) or Pipe (|) exports avoid parser breaks.
  • Formula Prefix Safety: Sanitizing cells beginning with =, +, -, or @ stops downstream analysts from triggering unintended macro or formula execution.
  • Date Format Serialization: Excel stores dates internally as floating-point serial numbers (days since 1900); export converts dates into standardized ISO-8601 (YYYY-MM-DD) strings so databases parse timestamps cleanly.

Common Pitfalls & Engineering Troubleshooting

Excel Serial Date Number Corruption

Excel internally represents dates as floating-point serial numbers counting days since January 1, 1900 (for example, January 1, 2023 is stored as 44927). Naive converters export these raw numeric serials rather than formatted date strings. TabKit decodes cell format masks to output human-readable dates.

Formula Values vs Calculation Cache Inconsistencies

If an Excel workbook was modified by third-party software without triggering recalculation, the cached formula results may be stale or empty. TabKit evaluates calculated values to ensure data fidelity in exported CSVs.

Number Formatting and Currency Symbol Pollution

Cells formatted as currency ($1,234.56) or percentages (12.5%) can cause database integer/float ingestion errors if the currency symbols and commas are exported as literal text. Always verify numeric column cleaning.
